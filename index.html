Learn — settlement & sports

New to settlement? Read this once to get your bearings — what settling actually means, and the sports you'll see — then head to Start for a practice quiz. These are general concepts to orient you; always follow Kambi's official settlement rules for the exact wording and edge cases.

What settlement means ⌃

Settlement is resolving bets after an event has been played. Once the official result is known, every bet offer on the event is marked so that winning bets pay out and losing bets don't.

Your job is to read the result from a trusted source and apply it correctly to each market. Accuracy matters more than speed — a wrong settlement pays the wrong people, and correcting it afterwards is costly.

The outcomes you'll apply ⌃
  • Winner — the outcome that actually happened. Bets on it win; bets on the other outcomes lose.
  • Void — the offer is cancelled and stakes are returned. Used when a market can't stand (event abandoned, or a required participant doesn't take part).
  • No result — the market has no valid result to settle against. It's not the same as void; apply it only where the market's rules call for it.
  • Postponed — the event didn't take place as scheduled. Depending on the rules, bets either stand until it's replayed or are voided.
How an event page is laid out ⌃

Every event you settle in this tool has the same sections — the same ones you'll practise on:

  • Postponements — mark here if the whole event is called off.
  • Results — the figures the markets settle from (e.g. goals and corners, first half and full time).
  • Player Occurrences — the starting line-ups plus events like goals, substitutions and own goals.
  • Bet Offers — the individual markets; tick the winning outcome, or Void, or No result.
Football ⌃
The game

Football (soccer) is played between two teams of eleven over two halves of 45 minutes plus added time. A goal is scored when the whole ball crosses the line into the net; the team with more goals wins, and a level score is a draw. Players line up as a goalkeeper, defenders, midfielders and forwards, and each side can make a limited number of substitutions. Other things that get recorded and bet on include corners, yellow and red cards, penalties and own goals. In knockout formats a draw can go to extra time and then a penalty shoot-out.

Settling it

Common markets: 1X2 (Home / Draw / Away), Double Chance, Both Teams To Score, Over/Under goals, corners, correct score, and half-specific versions.

Periods matter. A "1st Half" market settles on the half-time score, not full time — one of the easiest mistakes to make.

Player occurrences track goals, substitutions and own goals per player. An own goal counts for the opposing team — it adds to their score, not the scorer's, which is why in this tool an own goal shows on the other team's side.

Watch for: settling the wrong period, and putting an own goal on the wrong side.

Golf ⌃
The game

Golf is an individual sport: each player tries to get the ball into a series of holes — usually 18 per round — in as few strokes as possible. A professional tournament is normally four rounds played over four days, and the player with the lowest total strokes wins, so it's a whole field competing rather than two sides head-to-head. Every hole has a par (the expected number of strokes); one under par is a birdie, two under an eagle, and one over a bogey. After two rounds a cut often removes the lower-ranked players, and a live leaderboard shows everyone's position.

Settling it

Most tournaments are stroke play — the lowest total strokes wins, and a player's placement is their finishing position.

Common markets: outright winner, Top 5 / Top 10 / Top 20 finishes, each-way, and matchups (two or three players — whoever finishes higher).

Ties are the tricky part. Some matchups are Tie No Bet (stake returned if players finish level). Outright and top-N markets often use dead-heat rules, where the payout is divided when players tie for a place. Players who miss the cut or withdraw are settled per the market's rules.

Watch for: dead-heat situations and tie handling on matchups.

More sports (quick intros) ⌃

You'll mostly settle football and golf here, but the same ideas carry across sports. Quick "how it's played" primers:

  • Tennis — one player (or pair) versus another, hitting a ball over a net into the opponent's court; scoring builds points → games → sets → match. Retirements and walkovers have their own void rules.
  • Basketball — two teams of five score by shooting into a hoop (2 or 3 points, 1 for a free throw); highest score wins. Check whether a market includes overtime.
  • Baseball — two teams alternate batting and fielding over nine innings; runs are scored by completing the bases. Rain and extra innings can affect settlement.
  • Ice hockey — two teams put a puck into the net over three periods; a tie may go to overtime or a shoot-out depending on the competition.
  • Cricket — two teams take turns batting and bowling, scoring runs against wickets; formats range from a few hours (T20) to multi-day Tests, which changes how markets settle.
  • American football — two teams of eleven advance an oval ball to score touchdowns (6) and field goals (3) over four quarters; overtime rules vary by competition.

These are orientation only — always settle to the specific market's rules.

Common pitfalls ⌃
  • Confirm the event and date — kick-off times and time zones cause mix-ups.
  • Settling the wrong period (half vs full time).
  • Crediting an own goal to the wrong team.
  • Confusing Void with No result — they behave differently.
  • Dead heats and ties, especially in golf and matchups.
This primer covers general concepts to get you oriented. The exact rules for void, no result, dead heats and postponements vary by market and are defined by Kambi's official settlement rules — always defer to those.
Guide

What this is

A training replica of the settlement back-office. You settle practice events, then see where you went right or wrong. All data is sample data. New to settlement? Read the Learn tab first for a sports and settlement primer.

Settling an event

  • Postponements — if the whole event is called off, tick Entire Event and SAVE. That postpones everything and closes the event; you skip the rest.
  • Results — type the goals and corners for First half and Full Time.
  • Player Occurrences — tick the starting XI for each team, then log any goals, substitutions and own goals. Tick both “log complete” boxes when done.
  • Bet Offers — tick the winning outcome, or the single Void box, then SAVE. Settled offers disappear. Use Settled to review ones you’ve done, and the search box to find an offer.

An event only closes once every section is answered. When all events are done, your Results appear automatically.

Creating events

In Create, build an event and set its correct answers — that answer key is what your settlement is graded against. Use Export/Import to move events between machines.
